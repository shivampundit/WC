Chapter 01: The IFSCA Act, 2019: Statutory Architecture (Sections 1 to 34 & Schedules)

Enactment, Composition of Authority, Unified Regulator Powers (Sec 12–13), Foreign Currency Mandate (Sec 20) & IFSCA Fund

🔥 High-Yield Statutory Norms, 2025–2026 Regulatory Updates & Exam Triggers
  • Enactment & Commencement: The International Financial Services Centres Authority Act, 2019 (Act No. 50 of 2019) received Presidential assent on December 19, 2019; IFSCA was formally established on April 27, 2020 with headquarters at GIFT City, Gandhinagar, Gujarat.
  • Unified Regulator (First Schedule): Under Section 13 read with the First Schedule, IFSCA exercises all powers of 4 domestic financial sector regulators — RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and PFRDA — in respect of financial products, financial services, and financial institutions in an IFSC.
  • Section 20 (Transactions in Foreign Currency): Every transaction of financial services in an IFSC shall be in such foreign currency as may be specified by regulations in consultation with the Central Government (with INR permitted only for administrative expenses or specified settlement).
  • Current Leadership & Setup (2026): Chairperson appointed under Section 5 holds office for a term of 3 years or up to age 65 years (whichever is earlier) and is eligible for reappointment.

📖 In-Depth Conceptual & Statutory Breakdown

1. Statutory Structure of the IFSCA Act, 2019 (7 Chapters, 34 Sections, 2 Schedules)

Before the enactment of the IFSCA Act, 2019, entities operating in India's first International Financial Services Centre (GIFT IFSC, notified as a Multi-Services SEZ under Section 18 of the SEZ Act, 2005) were regulated separately by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and PFRDA. To provide a unified, agile regulatory environment comparable to London, Singapore, Dubai (DIFC), and Hong Kong, Parliament enacted the IFSCA Act, 2019.

  • Chapter I (Sections 1–3 — Preliminary): Short title, extent (whole of India), definitions of 'Financial Institution' [Sec 3(1)(c)], 'Financial Product' [Sec 3(1)(d)], and 'Financial Service' [Sec 3(1)(e)].
  • Chapter II (Sections 4–11 — Establishment of Authority): Section 4 establishes IFSCA as a body corporate; Section 5 specifies composition (Chairperson + 1 Member each nominated by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA + 2 Officials of Ministry of Finance + 2 other Members appointed on recommendation of a Selection Committee); Section 6 sets term at 3 years / 65 years of age.
  • Chapter III (Sections 12–13 — Functions and Powers of Authority): Section 12 mandates IFSCA to develop and regulate financial products, financial services, and financial institutions in an IFSC; Section 13 transfers powers of domestic regulators listed in the First Schedule (RBI Act 1934, Banking Regulation Act 1949, FEMA 1999, PSS Act 2007, SEBI Act 1992, SCRA 1956, Depositories Act 1996, Insurance Act 1938, IRDAI Act 1999, PFRDA Act 2013) to IFSCA for IFSC units.
  • Chapter IV (Sections 14–19 — Finance, Accounts and Audit): Central Government grants (Sec 14), IFSCA Fund (Sec 15), Accounts and Audit by C&AG (Sec 16), Performance Review Committee (Sec 17 — constituted by IFSCA consisting of at least 2 members of the Authority to review compliance with applicable laws at least once every financial year).
  • Chapter V (Sections 20–21 — Foreign Exchange Transactions): Section 20 mandates foreign currency transactions; Section 21 empowers Central Government to set up additional Appellate Tribunals or designate existing ones.
  • Chapter VI & VII (Sections 22–34 — Miscellaneous): Power of Central Government to issue directions (Sec 23), supersede Authority for up to 6 months (Sec 24), power to make Rules (Sec 27) and Regulations (Sec 28), and power to modify/exempt provisions of other Acts in their application to IFSC by notification (Sec 31).

2. Deep Dive: Definitions of Financial Product [Sec 3(1)(d)] & Financial Service [Sec 3(1)(e)]

Examiners in IFSCA Grade A frequently test whether an item is classified as a 'Financial Product' under Section 3(1)(d) or a 'Financial Service' under Section 3(1)(e), including newly notified additions by the Central Government.

  • Financial Products [Section 3(1)(d)]: (i) Securities; (ii) Contracts of Insurance; (iii) Deposits; (iv) Credit arrangements (including Operating Lease & Financial Lease for Aircraft/Ships); (v) Foreign currency contracts other than spot physical delivery; and any other product notified by the Central Government.
  • Financial Services [Section 3(1)(e)]: (i) Buying, selling, or subscribing to a financial product; (ii) Acceptance of deposits; (iii) Safeguarding and administering assets (Custodial services); (iv) Effecting contracts of insurance; (v) Offering, managing, or operating a collective investment scheme / fund; (vi) Establishing or operating an investment scheme (PMS/AIF); (vii) Maintaining or transferring records of ownership; (viii) Underwriting; (ix) Providing information on creditworthiness; (x) Operating an exchange, clearing corporation, or depository; (xi) Global In-House Centres (GIC), Bookkeeping, Accounting, Taxation, and Financial Crime Compliance Services (BATF).

Chapter 02: GIFT City, GIFT IFSC & Special Economic Zone (SEZ) Framework

Multi-Services SEZ Architecture, Domestic Tariff Area (DTA) vs IFSC Zone, Dual Regulation Removal & Tax Incentives (Sec 80LA)

🔥 High-Yield Statutory Norms, 2025–2026 Regulatory Updates & Exam Triggers
  • Dual-Zone Structure of GIFT City: Spread across 886 acres in Gandhinagar, Gujarat, divided into (1) Domestic Tariff Area (DTA — ~625 acres) for domestic entities and (2) Multi-Services SEZ (PA / IFSC Zone — ~261 acres + expanded SEZ notification) for offshore/IFSC units.
  • FEMA Status of IFSC Units: Under FEMA (International Financial Services Centre) Regulations, 2015, any financial institution or branch set up in an IFSC is treated as a 'Person Resident Outside India' (Non-Resident / Offshore entity) for exchange control purposes!
  • Single-Window Clearance (SWITS): IFSCA launched the Single Window IT System (SWITS) integrating SEZ online approvals (Ministry of Commerce) with IFSCA licensing and registration.
  • Tax Holiday under Section 80LA (Income-tax Act): 100% income-tax deduction for any 10 consecutive assessment years out of a block of 15 years for IFSC units, plus 9% Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) (or nil under new regime), 0% GST on services provided to IFSC/offshore clients, and STT/CTT/Stamp Duty exemptions on IFSC exchanges.

📖 In-Depth Conceptual & Statutory Breakdown

1. Legal & Regulatory Architecture of GIFT IFSC under SEZ Act, 2005 & FEMA, 1999

Gujarat International Finance Tec-City (GIFT City) is a greenfield smart financial centre conceptualized in 2007 and developed by GIFTCL. Under Section 18(1) of the Special Economic Zones (SEZ) Act, 2005, the Central Government may approve the setting up of an International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) in a SEZ and prescribe requirements for setting up and operation of such centre.

  • Deemed Foreign Territory / Non-Resident Status: Although physically located in Gujarat, India, an IFSC unit is treated as a non-resident (offshore jurisdiction) under FEMA, 1999. Transactions between an Indian resident (in DTA) and an IFSC unit are treated as cross-border (foreign exchange) transactions.
  • Unified Development Control Powers: To eliminate dual approval friction between the Development Commissioner (SEZ Act) and IFSCA, the Ministry of Commerce delegated powers of the Development Commissioner for IFSC financial units to the Administrator (IFSCA).
  • International Arbitration & Dispute Resolution: The Gujarat International Maritime Arbitration Centre (GIMAC) and the proposed International Arbitration Centre at GIFT City provide rapid commercial dispute resolution aligned with international standards.

2. Comprehensive Tax & Fiscal Incentives for GIFT IFSC Entities (Union Budget Updates)

To make GIFT IFSC cost-competitive against Singapore, Dubai (DIFC), and Mauritius, the Union Budgets have progressively expanded direct and indirect tax incentives under the Income-tax Act, 1961.

  • Section 80LA Tax Holiday: 100% tax exemption on business profits for 10 consecutive years out of 15 years (at the option of the taxpayer) from the year of obtaining IFSCA registration.
  • Capital Gains Exemptions [Sec 47(viiab)]: Transfer of specified securities (foreign company shares, GDRs, rupee denominated bonds, derivatives, bullion depository receipts, units of investment funds) by a non-resident on a recognized stock exchange in IFSC is NOT treated as a 'transfer' and is 100% exempt from Capital Gains Tax.
  • Category I & II AIF Tax Pass-Through & Relocation (Tax Neutrality): Relocation of offshore funds from overseas jurisdictions (like Mauritius/Cayman/Singapore) to an IFSC Resultant Fund is completely tax-neutral; Category III AIFs in IFSC specified as 'Specified Funds' under Sec 10(4D) enjoy exemption on income from non-resident investors.
  • Aircraft & Ship Leasing Exemptions [Sec 10(4F) & 10(15)]: Royalty or interest paid by an IFSC unit to a non-resident for leasing of an aircraft or a ship is 100% exempt from withholding tax, and capital gains on transfer of leased aircraft/ships by an IFSC unit are exempt under Sec 80LA.

  • Factoring & Forfaiting / ITFS: IBUs participate actively on International Trade Financing Services (ITFS) platforms (such as RXIL Global, Vayana, M1NXT, KredX) to finance global trade receivables and payables.
  • Global Administrative Office (GAO) / Representative Office: Banks can also establish a GAO or Representative Office in IFSC for regional management, research, and administrative support without taking deposits.

📖 In-Depth Conceptual & Statutory Breakdown

1. Ecosystem of India International Bullion Exchange (IIBX) at GIFT IFSC

India is one of the world's largest consumers of gold (~800–850 tonnes/year) and silver, yet lacked an international price-discovery bullion exchange comparable to London (LBMA), Shanghai (SGE), or New York (COMEX). To establish India as a price-setter rather than a price-taker in precious metals, IFSCA notified the Bullion Exchange Regulations, 2020.

  • 4 Pillars of the Bullion Ecosystem: (1) Bullion Exchange (IIBX), (2) Bullion Clearing Corporation, (3) Bullion Depository (IIDI), and (4) Vault Managers (such as Sequel, Brinks, Loomis operating SEZ-bonded vaults).
  • Bullion Depository Receipts (BDRs): Physical gold or silver bars meeting OECD Due Diligence Guidelines for Responsible Supply Chains and LBMA / UAE Good Delivery standards are deposited by overseas suppliers/bullion banks into an IFSCA-registered Vault Manager. The Bullion Depository (IIDI) issues electronic **Bullion Depository Receipts (BDRs)**, which are traded in USD on IIBX.
  • Settlement & Delivery: Trades in BDRs on IIBX settle on a **T+0 (Within 30 minutes / near-real-time)** basis! When a Qualified Jeweller or Nominated Bank buys BDRs, it extinguishes the BDR to take physical delivery of the gold/silver after paying applicable customs duty.

2. Qualified Jewellers (QJs), Qualified Suppliers & UAE CEPA TRQ Mechanism

Historically, only RBI-nominated banks and nominated agencies could import gold into India. IIBX democratized bullion imports by introducing the 'Qualified Jeweller' and 'Qualified Supplier' categories.

  • Qualified Jeweller (QJ) Eligibility: An Indian entity engaged in the business of bullion/jewellery with a minimum net worth of **₹15 Crore / ₹25 Crore** (as per IFSCA circulars) and 90% turnover in bullion/jewellery over the preceding financial years can be notified as a Qualified Jeweller to buy BDRs on IIBX for domestic import.
  • India-UAE CEPA Tariff Rate Quota (TRQ): Under the India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), gold imported under the TRQ enjoys a 1% customs duty concession and is routed transparently through IIBX.
  • Sovereign Gold Bonds vs BDRs: While SGBs are government securities denominated in grams of gold issued by RBI onshore, BDRs are electronic warehouse receipts backed 1:1 by physical allocated gold/silver bars (995 / 999 purity, 1 kg or 100 gm gold bars; 30 kg silver bars) stored in IFSC vaults.

📖 In-Depth Conceptual & Statutory Breakdown

1. LIBOR-to-SOFR Transition, Basel III Endgame & Global Banking Turmoil Lessons (SVB / Credit Suisse)

Global financial shocks transmit to emerging economies through four channels: the **Trade Channel**, the **Financial / Portfolio Flow Channel ('Taper Tantrum' / FPI outflows)**, the **Exchange Rate & Imported Inflation Channel**, and the **Confidence / Contagion Channel**.

  • Why LIBOR Was Replaced by SOFR / SONIA / €STR: LIBOR was a *forward-looking, unsecured, quote-based* rate vulnerable to bank panel rigging. **SOFR (USA)** is a *backward-looking, overnight, transaction-based* rate secured by **U.S. Treasury Repo transactions**. In India, Financial Benchmarks India Pvt Ltd (**FBIL**) transitioned foreign currency derivatives from MIFOR (which used USD LIBOR) to **Modified MIFOR (using SOFR)**.
  • Lessons from the 2023 US Regional Bank (SVB) & Credit Suisse Crisis: Highlighted **Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book (IRRBB)** on Held-to-Maturity (HTM) bond portfolios when central banks hike rates rapidly, the speed of digital social-media bank runs, and the write-down of **Additional Tier-1 (AT-1) Perpetual Bonds** (Basel III Point of Non-Viability — PONV trigger).
  • Basel III Capital Adequacy in India (RBI vs BCBS): While the Basel Committee (BCBS) prescribes a minimum CRAR of **8% + 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) = 10.5%**, RBI mandates a stricter minimum CRAR of **9% + 2.5% CCB = 11.5%** (plus an additional **0.60% / 0.80% D-SIB surcharge** for **SBI, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank**!).

📖 In-Depth Conceptual & Statutory Breakdown

1. Bank vs NBFC Distinctions & The 4-Layer Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) Architecture

While NBFCs perform credit intermediation parallel to banks ('Shadow Banking'), they differ from commercial banks in three fundamental legal respects under the RBI Act, 1934 and Banking Regulation Act, 1949:

  • 3 Fundamental Differences between NBFCs and Banks: (1) NBFCs **CANNOT accept Demand Deposits** (Savings or Current Accounts); (2) NBFCs **do NOT form part of the Payment and Settlement System** and **cannot issue cheques drawn on themselves**; and (3) Deposits with Deposit-taking NBFCs (NBFC-D) are **NOT insured by DICGC**!
  • Harmonized NPA Recognition (90-Day Overdue Norm) & Capital Adequacy: Under SBR, all NBFCs (including Base Layer via glide path) follow the **90-day overdue norm** for Non-Performing Asset (NPA) classification! Minimum Capital Adequacy (**CRAR = 15% of Risk-Weighted Assets** for NBFC-ML and NBFC-UL, with **Tier-1 Capital >= 10%** for general NBFCs and **Common Equity Tier-1 CET-1 >= 9%** for Upper Layer NBFCs!).

2. Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework for NBFCs & Specialized NBFC Categories

Following the IL&FS and DHFL crises, RBI extended its **Prompt Corrective Action (PCA)** framework to all Deposit-Taking NBFCs (NBFC-D) and Non-Deposit NBFCs in the Middle, Upper, and Top Layers (excluding Govt NBFCs initially, then extended to Government NBFCs from October 2024!).

  • 3 Risk Threshold Indicators for NBFC PCA: (1) **Capital Adequacy (CRAR)** & **Tier-1 Capital Ratio** (Risk Threshold 1 triggered if CRAR falls below 15% down to 12%, or Tier-1 falls below 10% down to 8%); and (2) **Asset Quality — Net Non-Performing Assets Ratio (NNPA)** (Risk Threshold 1 triggered if **NNPA > 6% up to 9%**, Threshold 2 if **> 9% up to 12%**, Threshold 3 if **> 12%**!).
  • Specialized NBFC Types: (1) **NBFC-MFI (Microfinance)**: At least **75% of total assets** deployed in qualifying microfinance loans (defined cleanly by RBI as a **collateral-free loan given to a household having annual household income up to ₹3,00,000**, where total monthly loan repayments across all lenders are capped at **50% of monthly household income**!); (2) **NBFC-IFC (Infrastructure Finance Company)**: Min **75% of total assets** in infrastructure loans, Min NOF **₹300 Crore**, CRAR 15%; (3) **Core Investment Company (CIC-ND-SI)**: Asset size >= ₹100 Cr, invests at least **90% of net assets** in group company shares/bonds/loans (of which at least **60% in equity shares**).
